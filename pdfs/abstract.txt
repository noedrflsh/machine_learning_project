This study investigates two fundamental machine learning questions using the airline passenger satisfaction benchmark ($N=699{,}635$): (1) whether the numerical spacing of ordinal rating scales impacts model predictions, and (2) how training-data corruption type, rate, and location affect model robustness. Our empirical evaluation across 100 model fits demonstrates that tree-based ensembles (Decision Trees and Random Forests) remain completely invariant to strictly monotonic ordinal transformations ($\Delta\text{ROC-AUC} \equiv 0$, $0.00\%$ flips). In contrast, linear and margin-based models (Logistic Regression and Linear SVM) exhibit substantial probability drift, classification volatility ($>3.5\%$ prediction flips under exponential scaling), and performance gains ($+1.17$ pp ROC-AUC) due to nonlinear basis expansion. Furthermore, we analyze feature corruption vulnerabilities and boundary sensitivity.
